# Lessons Learned

Accumulated lessons from running the pipeline. Add new lessons as they arise.

## Photo galleries (layout)
- **Photo galleries: use a CSS `grid`, never CSS `columns`.** CSS multi-column balances children by count, not height, so a gallery whose photo count doesn't divide evenly across the columns leaves a big empty void in the short column. A June 2026 fleet scan found this live on 20 of 98 deployed sites (~20%), worst cases a third-height column, all of which had passed QA. A `grid` can't do it (worst case = a benign partial last row). Use `grid grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-3` with uniform-aspect cells (majority orientation); for the masonry look, grid + portrait `row-span-2` + `grid-auto-flow: dense`. Never `columns` for photos (it stays fine for variable-height TEXT cards like testimonials). QA now runs a deterministic void measurement (worst empty-column gap as a % of gallery height) and hard-fails >=18%; crucially it catches the void however the masonry was authored (`columns-N`, `[column-count:N]`, a custom `.masonry` class), which a source grep misses (of the 18 it flagged, ~3 used `[column-count:N]` or a custom `.masonry` class rather than a literal `columns-N`).

## Region-Specific Sources

Klaudius's `gather` skill ships with a universal core — Google Maps, Yahoo Search, Instagram, Facebook, Apple Maps, Restaurant Guru, Wanderlog, TripAdvisor — that works in every country. Country-specific business directories, review aggregators, and registries are *not* curated by Klaudius (they go stale fast and we'd rather not push outdated guidance). Instead, you (Claude) accumulate that regional knowledge in the Site Access Status section below as you discover it while running the pipeline: which directory/aggregator covers the operator's country well (with access notes — Playwright vs. Patchright vs. fully Cloudflare-blocked), which ones are dead or unreliable, and any industry-specific platforms that dominate locally for trades / beauty / food / etc. The `gather` skill reads this file at the start of every invocation, so each entry you write becomes operational intelligence for the next run.

## Photos
- **Instagram CDN URLs expire within hours.** Never hotlink cdninstagram.com URLs. Download to `site/public/images/` and reference as `/images/filename.jpg`. Google Maps and Restaurant Guru URLs are permanent.
- **Google Maps cover photos can be wrong.** If a business has fewer than 3 Google Maps photos, the cover photo may be auto-assigned by Google and show a completely different business (e.g. hair salon photo on a cleaning company).
- **Street View can be unflattering.** Shutters down, vans blocking, wrong building. Take a screenshot and visually verify before including.
- **Restaurant Guru photos have a cartoon watermark.** Use as fallback when no better source exists, but prefer Google Maps or Instagram photos.
- **Agents describe photos instead of extracting URLs.** Writing "Instagram has 25 posts with photos" is useless - the build step needs actual URLs or downloaded files.
- **Verify Instagram downloads before using.** Not every Instagram post is a usable photo. Posts can be closure notices, text announcements, memes, or promotional graphics. After downloading each image, visually verify it with the Read tool. If it's not a real photo of the business or their work, delete it and find another post.
- **Don't use generic Street View as filler.** Random photos of the town centre, car parks, or nearby shops add no value to a business website. Only use Street View if it clearly shows the actual business premises. Better to have fewer photos than irrelevant ones.
- **Always download Google Maps photos at full resolution.** When downloading from `lh5.googleusercontent.com` or `lh3.googleusercontent.com`, use `=w0-h0` suffix (not `=w800-h600-k-no` or similar). The `=w0-h0` gives the original full-resolution image. Smaller suffixes produce blurry, pixelated photos that look terrible on the deployed site, especially in full-width hero/background sections. Example: `curl -sL "https://lh5.googleusercontent.com/p/XXXXX=w0-h0" -o photo.jpg`.
- **Check photo cropping.** Photos displayed with `object-cover` can crop the main subject. If the subject is off-centre or the photo is mostly background, either adjust `object-position` or use a different photo.
- **Most Google/Maps business photos are PORTRAIT 3:4 — frame to orientation, never slice.** `places-photos.js` now prints each photo's `WxH (orientation)` as it downloads; check it before laying a photo out. Put portrait photos in portrait containers (`aspect-[3/4]` or `aspect-[4/5]`) and landscape photos in wide ones (`aspect-[4/3]`/`aspect-[16/10]`), with `<img className="h-full w-full object-cover object-center">` inside the wrapper. NEVER force a portrait photo into a short, wide fixed-height box (`h-72`, `h-[220px]`, a half-width card capped at `h-72`, a `h-[220px]` slot in a gallery column, etc.) — `object-cover` then shows only a thin horizontal band and slices the subject. This is the classic "why is this photo so heavily cropped" failure. The "just give it enough height (`h-[420px]`)" instinct is the trap: a fixed height on a wide container is exactly what crops a portrait. Prefer `aspect-[X/Y]` wrappers sized to the photo over any fixed `h-[...]`. And when most photos are portrait, lean into it — an editorial layout (a tall feature image beside a list, a portrait "work" trio, photos woven through sections) flatters them and looks far less templated than a uniform image-top card grid.
- **Facebook full-res photos: scrape the TIMELINE render, not the photos grid.** Logged-out, the `/photos` grid and `photo.php?fbid=` permalinks are login-walled or serve 206px thumbnails (the `ctp=s206x206` param in the `t39.30808` URL governs the served size, and the `oh=` signature is URL-bound so you can't just edit the param up). BUT main-timeline post images render at a larger `ctp` (e.g. `ctp=s960x960`) and those exact URLs download fine via plain `curl` (fbcdn blocks curl on profile-pic `og:image` but NOT on these `t39.30808-6` content images). So: `npx playwright-cli goto <fb-page>`, scroll a few times, extract `img.src` where `naturalWidth>=480`, curl those. A business with 0 Google Maps photos and a login-walled FB can still yield 1+ usable hero photos this way (got Noble Gas Wrexham's bathroom shot at 720x960 when every other source was empty). Reshares/video posts won't yield stills — expect only the genuine work photos.
- **Facebook photos are low-res without login.** The photos grid on public Facebook profiles only serves thumbnails (max ~851x315). Individual photo pages require login. These are usable but noticeably soft at larger display sizes. Prefer Google Maps or Instagram photos when available; use Facebook photos as a fallback rather than primary images.
- **Use `places-photos.js` for Google Maps photos.** `node scripts/places-photos.js "Business Name Location" OUTPUT_DIR --max=10` searches the Places API, fetches photo references, and downloads them at 1200px. No browser needed — bypasses the Google Maps consent wall entirely. This is faster and more reliable than any Playwright/Patchright approach.
- **Phone-screenshot work photos often have BLACK LETTERBOX BARS top/bottom — crop them before use.** Trades frequently upload 9:16 phone screenshots to Google Maps/Facebook where the real photo is letterboxed inside black bars (McPlumb & Heating's boiler/bathroom shots were all like this). `object-cover` in an `aspect-[X/Y]` cell can still show a black band, and QA/hero screenshots then look broken. Auto-crop with PIL: scan rows from the edges and trim the sustained near-black band. CAVEAT: a simple row-average-brightness check FAILS on iOS screenshots because the light grey home-indicator pill at the very bottom raises that row's average above the black threshold, so the crop stops one row in and leaves the whole band. Instead detect the START of the trailing dark run (first y where the next ~25 rows are all >90% dark pixels) and crop above it, or use a dark-pixel-fraction test that tolerates the pill. Re-Read the cropped image to confirm the band is gone.
- **External images can appear broken during local QA but work fine once deployed.** `npx serve` (used for local QA) is a bare-bones static file server that differs from a production CDN (Vercel, Cloudflare, or Netlify) in referrer/CORS handling. Google Maps `lh3.googleusercontent.com` images sometimes render as 0x0 in headless browsers on localhost but load perfectly on the live deployment. Always verify with `curl` before removing — if curl returns HTTP 200 with reasonable file size, the image works. Do NOT restructure page layouts to remove "broken" images that are actually fine in production.

## Deployment
The `/deploy` skill is the source of truth and branches on `DEPLOY_PROVIDER` (Vercel, Cloudflare Pages, or Netlify). These lessons apply to all hosts.
- **Always reuse the same deploy project (the client slug).** When redeploying, target the exact same project name. Check Supabase for the existing deployed_url: `python3 scripts/db.py client SLUG`. Never create duplicate projects with suffixes.
- **If deployment fails (rate limit, quota, auth/403 error), STOP.** Do not fall back to the other host, GitHub Pages, or any other platform. Mark the client as "built" in Supabase and do NOT send outreach. Wait for the user to resolve the issue (on Cloudflare a 403 usually means the API token is missing the "Cloudflare Pages → Edit" permission). Sending outreach with a broken or non-standard URL wastes the lead.

## Outreach
- **SMS is the fallback for businesses without email.** If a business has a mobile number but no email, send outreach via SMS using `python3 scripts/imessage.py send` (or `python3 scripts/twilio_sms.py send` if you configured Twilio). Only mark as `unreachable` if the number is a landline AND no email/social media exists.
- **Sound human, not AI.** No em dashes, no filler phrases ("I wanted to reach out"), use contractions, keep it under 150 words.
- **Don't combine compliments that backfire.** "39 reviews after 25 years" implies they should have more. Keep review count and years in business as separate compliments.
- **Be precise about facts.** Review counts, star ratings, years in business - the owner knows these intimately and will notice if wrong.
- **Log ALL outreach immediately** including manual sends (social media DMs, contact forms). Supabase is the single source of truth for pipeline state.
- **Always check Supabase before sending any outreach.** Even if an email bounced, a previous session may have already resent via SMS. Check the client's current outreach status and channel before sending to avoid duplicates: `python3 scripts/db.py client SLUG`.
- **Check your email provider's Sent folder before sending any email.** Before sending a follow-up email, search the Sent folder for emails TO that client sent today. If one already exists, skip it. This prevents duplicate sends when batch scripts overlap or get restarted. Same applies to SMS — check chat.db (iMessage) or Twilio history for outgoing messages today before sending.
- **Update Supabase immediately after ANY manual outreach.** If the user sends Instagram DMs, Facebook messages, or any manual outreach, update those clients to `outreach_sent` in Supabase right away. Otherwise other sessions will see them as incomplete and try to re-outreach.
- **Always put the mobile number in the `phone` field.** The `phone` field is used for SMS outreach. If only a landline exists, put it in `phone`. If both exist, put mobile in `phone` and landline in `landline`. Never store a landline in `phone` when a mobile is available - it causes SMS sends to fail.

## Data Gathering
- **Verify the Places API actually returned YOUR business, not a similarly-named one.** `places:searchText` returns results loosely matching the query. With similar local-trade names (e.g. "A.D.S Painter & Decorator" vs "D A Decorating Services" — both painters in the same town, both small local outfits) Google may rank the wrong one at #1. The agent then writes that CID into gathered-content.md, and the build step embeds the wrong Google Maps listing, attributes the wrong reviews, and produces a site about Business X branded as Business Y. The contact mobile sourced separately (e.g. from Facebook) may be correct, but everything Maps-derived is wrong — including the live business the recipient sees when they click the embedded map. The scripts now request the top 5 API results and auto-pick the first one whose `displayName` matches the query's BRAND + INDUSTRY descriptor (token-level substring match against a tight-normalised form). When the right business is at #2 you'll see a `Note: picked result #2 because #1 ("...") did not match` line — proceed normally; that's the disambiguation working. Only when none of the top 5 match does the script exit non-zero with `MISMATCH` — at that point the business almost certainly isn't on Maps under the queried name, and you should skip the candidate rather than retry with a fuzzier query (which will cheerfully return the wrong business and lock you back into the same failure mode).

- **Branded floor mats / vans in Google Maps photos often carry the contact details.** When you Read the downloaded `places-photos.js` images to vet them, watch for the business's own branded work mat, van livery, or signage in frame — trades frequently photograph a job with their mat on the floor, and it prints the phone, **email**, tagline, and social icons. Evotekk's email (`evotekk1@gmail.com`) was only discoverable this way (Google Maps showed no email, DDG was rate-limited, domains didn't resolve). A gmail/outlook address printed there (rather than a custom domain) is also a strong corroborating signal that the business genuinely has no website. So always actually look at the photos, don't just check resolution.

- **Two-pass contact check before building.** Pass 1: obvious sources. Pass 2: Instagram REST API, Booksy, Fresha, ThreeBestRated, Cylex. If both fail, skip the business.
- **Coverage matters, not volume.** A business with 15 reviews and no Instagram is fine if we capture all 15 reviews. The problem is when a business has 500 reviews and an active Instagram but we only grab 2 reviews and no photos. Don't skip businesses because they have a small online presence - build the best site possible from what exists.
- **Use `places-reviews.js` as the primary review source.** Run `node scripts/places-reviews.js "Business Name Location"` — it returns up to 5 reviews with full text, author name, rating, and date from the Google Places API in one call. This is far more reliable than scraping review aggregator sites (ReviewBritain, CityMaps) which frequently 404 or get Cloudflare-blocked. Use aggregator sites only as a backup if you need more than 5 reviews.
- **Google Maps does NOT show review counts or review text in headless browsers.** Even when signed in, headless Playwright gets Google's "limited view" which hides review counts and review text. Do NOT waste time trying to extract reviews from Google Maps (scrolling review sections, clicking tabs, trying different URLs). Use Google Maps ONLY for: rating (stars), address, phone, hours, photos, and confirming "Add website".

- **Aggregator sub-scores are NOT overall star ratings.** Restaurant Guru, TripAdvisor, and similar aggregators often show per-category sub-scores (Food / Service / Atmosphere / Value) next to reviews that were originally posted on Google. A reviewer can give Food: 5/5 while giving the business a 2-star OVERALL rating on Google — and the review text in that case is often short, narrowly worded, or damning with faint praise ("tasty food" and nothing else). When capturing reviews from aggregators, record the OVERALL star rating only — never treat a category sub-score as overall stars. If the overall rating isn't exposed, skip the review; we burned a lead once by showing a 2-star Google review as 5-star social proof because the aggregator only exposed Food: 5/5. `places-reviews.js` (Google Places API) is the safe primary source — it returns verified overall ratings.

- **"Unreachable" means NO contact method at all.** A business is only unreachable if there is no email, no mobile phone number, AND no social media presence (Facebook/Instagram). If they have a mobile number, use SMS. If they have Instagram/Facebook but no email or phone, proceed through the full pipeline and mark for manual DM — store the Facebook URL in the `facebook` column and/or Instagram handle in the `instagram` column in Supabase. Keep status as `deployed` (not `outreach_sent`) and add a note like "Manual DM required". Only set `outreach_sent` once the DM is actually sent.

## Building Sites
- **Always run `npx next build` in the foreground with `timeout: 120000`.** Never background it. The output file doesn't flush until the process finishes, so backgrounding just leads to 10+ wasted tool calls polling an empty file. Never run two builds concurrently in the same session — the second will corrupt `.next/` and both will fail.

- **Never poll with `pgrep -f` to wait for a process.** Watch loops like `while ps -p $(pgrep -f "next build")` or `until ! pgrep -f "outreach-notify-simple"` self-match: the polling shell's own command line contains the search pattern, so pgrep finds itself, the condition is permanently satisfied, and the watcher hangs forever. Long-running children alive for 19+ hours have come from this — they finished their actual work, then zombied on a post-action wait, leaking processes and (in some cases) leaving Supabase rows stuck mid-pipeline. If you must wait for a process you launched, capture its PID at launch (`some-cmd & PID=$!`) and `wait $PID`. Otherwise just run synchronously in the foreground. **The same self-match kills `pkill -f`**: `pkill -f "http.server $PORT"` matches the current compound shell command (whose own command line contains that string) and kills your shell mid-command — anything after it in the same Bash call (a heredoc write, a send) silently never runs (exit 144, observed 2026-07-23). Kill by captured PID, or walk `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` from a `pgrep` on the bare program name and match the port there. **The self-match also defeats the "safe" rewrite `for p in $(pgrep -f "http.server $PORT"); do kill $p; done`** — the enclosing `bash -c` command line contains that port string too, so the loop kills its own shell and everything later in the same Bash call is silently skipped (exit 144 again, observed 2026-07-25: the server did die, but the playwright `close` calls, the `node_modules` cleanup and the Telegram notify after it never ran). Filtering inside the loop by reading `/proc/$p/cmdline` does not help either, because the shell's own cmdline genuinely contains the port. Only two things are safe: capture the PID at launch (`python3 -m http.server $PORT & SERVE_PID=$!` then `kill $SERVE_PID`), or put the kill in its OWN Bash call whose command line does not mention the port (e.g. read the PID from a file written earlier).

- **Never use CSS grid for service/feature card layouts.** `grid grid-cols-3` left-aligns the last row when the item count isn't divisible by 3 (e.g. 5 cards leaves 2 awkwardly stuck to the left). Use flexbox instead: `flex flex-wrap justify-center gap-6` with explicit widths `w-full md:w-[calc(50%-12px)] lg:w-[calc(33.333%-16px)]`. This centres any incomplete last row automatically and works with any number of cards.

- **Load Google Fonts via `next/font/google`, never via `@import url(...)` in globals.css — Turbopack (Next 16) silently strips the external @import.** The built CSS keeps the `font-family: 'X'` declarations but the font files are never fetched, so every heading/body renders in the browser fallback (Times/Helvetica) on the LIVE site. The failure is invisible to source review AND to a QA agent that greps for the font names — l2r-plumbing-heating-morley shipped this way and QA passed it, praising a font pairing that wasn't actually rendering. Fix: `import { Font_Name } from "next/font/google"` in layout.tsx with `variable:` CSS vars, reference `var(--font-...)` in globals.css (this also self-hosts the woff2s = faster + no Google dependency). Verify visually: in the QA/hero screenshot, a heavy display font that renders as thin default serif = the font didn't load. Related: after `goto`, `document.fonts` can report "loaded" while the raster still shows the fallback — wait, re-screenshot, and zoom the headline crop to confirm the real face before attaching a hero screenshot to outreach.
- **Fonts must look bespoke, not AI-generated.** Never use generic fonts (Barlow, DM Sans, Poppins, Open Sans, Montserrat, Raleway — all banned). But also don't blindly trust ui-ux-pro-max's recommendation — its pairings can be "refined but safe" (e.g. Fraunces/Outfit, Instrument Serif/Sora) which still look like an AI picked them. After getting the recommendation, ask: "Would a human designer pick this, or is it predictable?" If it feels safe, search for something bolder — condensed slab serifs, heavy display faces, textured or unusual typefaces. The goal is fonts that look like a deliberate design choice, not a template default. More unique = more bespoke-looking.

- **Hide the mobile sticky CTA while the hero is visible.** Most sites have a "Call Now" button in the hero AND a fixed bottom bar with the phone number (`md:hidden`). On first load both are visible simultaneously, which looks redundant. Fix: use an `IntersectionObserver` on the hero section and toggle the sticky bar's opacity/pointer-events — hidden while the hero is on screen, fades in (300ms transition) once the user scrolls past it.

- **Tailwind v3 does NOT compile slash-opacity on arbitrary CSS-var colors.** `text-[var(--paper)]/70`, `bg-[var(--paper)]/5`, `border-[var(--paper)]/15` all silently become no-ops — the element ends up with no color rule and falls back to the body colour (black on dark sections = invisible). Named Tailwind colours with slash (`text-white/70`, `bg-black/40`) still work. For CSS-var colours, define explicit utility classes in globals.css: `.text-paper-70 { color: rgba(246,238,219,0.7); }` etc. and use those. The hero's main heading survives only because it uses the non-opacity variant `text-[var(--paper)]`; nav, credential strip, and footer copy using `/70` or `/80` will silently disappear.

- **Don't declare a default `color` on `h1, h2, h3, h4` in globals.css.** Combined with Tailwind's arbitrary-value color classes (specificity-equal but ordering-dependent), you can get headings that render the wrong colour on dark sections (near-black on near-black = invisible). Let headings inherit from their section's Tailwind text-color class, and apply colour with the Tailwind class on the specific heading where needed.

- **Register every palette colour in `tailwind.config.ts`, not just as CSS utility classes in globals.css.** If you define `.bg-char { background: var(--char) }` etc. in globals.css but don't add `char` to `theme.extend.colors`, then `bg-char` works but `from-char`, `to-char`, `via-char-80` in gradient utilities all silently no-op. Photo overlays built on `bg-gradient-to-b from-char via-char-80 to-char` render nothing, exposing the raw photo underneath. The failure is invisible in code review — `from-char` looks valid — and only shows up visually as "text clashes with photo". Fix: always mirror palette colours into `tailwind.config.ts` as concrete hex values (e.g. `char: "#1E1812"`, `ochre: "#C78A3D"`) so gradient stops resolve correctly. Utility classes in globals.css still serve a role (slash-opacity variants like `.bg-char-80`), but registered colours are the source of truth Tailwind reads for gradients.

- **Never add a "Scroll" cue, chevron, or blinking dot at the bottom of the hero.** It's AI-template cliché — users know how to scroll, the indicator earns no pixels, and it reads as "this site was generated by a tool". It also frequently breaks silently (e.g. the accompanying vertical line's `bg-plaster-20` utility class was never defined in globals.css, so only the word "SCROLL" floated there orphaned). If the hero needs a hint that more content follows, do it through the content itself — a peek of the next section visible at the fold, or a bottom gradient fade — not a literal text label.

- **If the navbar is fixed with a top offset (e.g. `top-[34px]`), every element above it MUST also be fixed/sticky.** A common pattern is an info strip (phone number, hours, "Est. 1987") sitting above the navbar in normal document flow, with the navbar offset down by the strip's height. On initial load it looks fine, but as soon as the user scrolls, the strip scrolls away and leaves an empty gap of exactly that offset between the top of the viewport and the navbar — the page background bleeds through and looks broken. Fix: either (a) make the top strip `fixed top-0 left-0 right-0 z-50` too so it pins alongside the navbar, or (b) drop the offset entirely and put the navbar at `top-0` with no strip above it. Never leave a fixed navbar floating below a non-fixed element.

- **`grid grid-cols-12 gap-N` causes mobile horizontal overflow.** When the grid has 12 tracks with `1fr` (i.e. `minmax(0, 1fr)`), each `gap-N` pixel value applies between EVERY track even when items span all 12 columns. With `gap-10` (40px) on a 12-track grid, the column gaps alone account for 11×40 = 440px — wider than a 393px iPhone viewport. Tracks collapse to 0px, but the gaps don't, and the grid's intrinsic content width exceeds the viewport. A `col-span-12` item then renders at 440px+ wide, its body text wraps to that wider width, and the user sees text cut off mid-word at the right edge. `overflow-x: clip` on `<html>`/`<body>` only hides the scroll — the wrap width is still wrong. Fix: split the gap into `gap-y-N md:gap-x-N md:gap-y-N` so column gap is 0 on mobile (where everything stacks vertically anyway) and only kicks in at md+ when items actually sit side by side. This applies to EVERY `grid grid-cols-12` in the page.

- **Don't combine `h-full` with `aspect-[X/Y]` on the same element.** They fight each other: aspect-ratio sets height from width, while h-full forces height = parent. On desktop they may agree by accident; on mobile (where the parent's height is unconstrained) the result is unpredictable and often produces images that overshoot their caption text in the next sibling, looking like an overlap bug. Pick one: aspect-ratio governs height, OR h-full does. Inside a flex column with a single child, the cleanest pattern is just `aspect-[X/Y] overflow-hidden` on the image wrapper and let height follow.

- **Marquee/ticker animations should run in the 18–28 second range.** A 2x-duplicated track translating -50% over 40s+ feels stuck — users read it as a broken/static element. Anything under 12s reads as dizzying. Default to ~22s. The same goes for any infinite-scroll horizontal strip.

- **Always set `html, body { overflow-x: clip; }` in globals.css.** Belt-and-braces: even with the gap fix above, a stray fixed-width element or wide image somewhere can re-introduce horizontal scroll. `overflow-x: clip` (preferred over `hidden` because it doesn't create a containing block for fixed children) prevents the page from ever scrolling horizontally. It does NOT fix wrong-wrap-width issues — those still need the underlying cause fixed — but it eliminates the visible double-wide-page failure mode if something slips through.

- **Heavy/condensed display fonts need `line-height ≥ 1.18`, never `≤ 1.0`.** Boldonse, Anton, Bebas Neue, Bungee, Archivo Black and similar heavy-weight or condensed display faces have unusually tall ascenders and deep descenders — at line-height 0.92–1.0 (which is the typical "tight display" default we generate), descenders from one line visibly intrude into the caps of the next, producing the "letters overlap" failure (observed where descenders from one line crashed into the caps of the next on a multi-line headline at mobile width). The bug is invisible in source review (`leading-[0.92]` looks fine) and only shows up on a rendered multi-line headline. Rule: when the chosen display font is heavy/condensed, set the global `.font-display` `line-height` to **1.20–1.25** and any per-element `leading-[X]` overrides to **≥ 1.18**, regardless of how much "tight display energy" the design wants. For lighter serifs and humanist sans-serifs (Spectral, Crimson Pro, Inter Tight) the usual 0.95–1.05 range is fine. Always render any multi-line headline at mobile width during QA — single-line desktop preview will not surface the overlap.

- **Never combine a fixed-height hero (`h-[X]` / `h-Xvh`) with `flex justify-end` and `overflow-hidden`.** This is the cut-off-headline bug: hero used `h-[78vh] min-h-[620px] max-h-[860px] overflow-hidden` plus an inner `h-full flex flex-col justify-end pb-14`. On phones with a short viewport (or any case where eyebrow + h1 + paragraph + CTAs exceeds container height minus pb), `justify-end` pushes content UP past the top edge of the photo container, where `overflow-hidden` clips it. The user sees the headline missing its first line (cut off mid-letter at the bottom of the navbar). The bug is invisible on the desktop viewports we usually QA on — it only triggers when content height > container height. Fix pattern: convert the photo container from fixed `h-[78vh]` to `min-h-[78vh] sm:min-h-[78vh]` (with a `min-h-[620px]` floor) and `flex flex-col`; convert the inner content div from `h-full` to `flex-1`; add a `pt-10 sm:pt-14` so even when the container grows, content has top breathing room. With this, the container expands when content needs it, and `justify-end` still pins to bottom without ever clipping. Always QA the hero at 393×800 (iPhone-ish short viewport), not just default desktop — short-viewport clipping is the failure mode that escapes review and lands on a real client.

- **Photo-backed heroes need a uniform dark (or light) wash across the ENTIRE image, not just directional edge gradients.** A gradient like `from-char via-transparent to-transparent` only darkens the left edge; warm or mid-toned photos in the middle/right of the hero will wash out cream/white text. Rule: over any hero background photo, apply a solid base overlay at 65–75% opacity covering 100% of the image, then add optional directional gradients on top to reinforce specific text zones (left-weighted for paragraph/CTA column, bottom fade for body copy). Never rely on edge-only gradients — the middle of the photo always fights the text. This also means the photo itself becomes atmospheric texture rather than competing for visual attention, which is what a hero photo should do. Concrete pattern: `<div absolute inset-0 bg-cover />` + `<div absolute inset-0 style={{ background: "rgba(R,G,B,0.72)" }} />` + optional `<div absolute inset-0 style={{ background: "linear-gradient(100deg, rgba(R,G,B,0.5) 0%, rgba(R,G,B,0.15) 55%, rgba(R,G,B,0.5) 100%)" }} />`.

## Finding Businesses
- **"No website" on Google Maps doesn't always mean no website.** Some businesses have a website but haven't linked it to their Google Maps listing. The gather skill's Step 0 now does a Yahoo search + domain variation checks. Domain guessing alone is not enough — businesses with long names often register shortened domains (e.g. a business called "Northern Window Cleaning Services" might own `northernwindowcleaning.co.uk`, dropping "Services"). The Yahoo search catches these because search engines index them even when Maps doesn't link them. Always run Step 0 before gathering, and for long business names, try truncated domain variations (drop trailing generic words like "cleaning", "services", "solutions").

## Playwright-cli Syntax
- **`npx playwright-cli eval` fails on arrow functions, `.map()`, `.forEach()`, spread syntax.** These all throw "not well-serializable" errors. Use old-style `for` loops, string concatenation, and `JSON.stringify()` wrappers instead. Example that WORKS: `npx playwright-cli eval "JSON.stringify(Array.from(document.querySelectorAll('img')).map(function(i) { return i.src }))"`. Example that FAILS: `npx playwright-cli eval "document.querySelectorAll('img').forEach(i => console.log(i.src))"`.

## Outreach Safety
- **Verify the hero screenshot shows YOUR client before attaching it.** Parallel sessions leave stale `python3 -m http.server` processes running; if you locate the server port via `ps`/`pgrep` instead of using the port you just bound, you can screenshot ANOTHER client's site (this nearly sent a Bath decorator's hero to a Winchester roofer, 2026-07-22). Always: bind your own known port, `curl -s localhost:$PORT | grep "<Business Name>"` to confirm, THEN screenshot, THEN Read the PNG to visually confirm it's the right business before composing the email.
- **Named playwright-cli sessions are machine-global — never use the outreach skill's literal `-s=outreach-hero` when parallel sessions may be running.** On 2026-07-23 two parallel pipeline sessions shared the `outreach-hero` session; the hero screenshot came back showing the OTHER session's client (Nelson Plumbing instead of RJG Joinery) even though the local curl-grep check passed (it checks the server, not the browser). Whoever navigated last wins, and a `close` kills the other session's browser too. Use a unique per-client session name (e.g. `-s=<slug>-hero`), and keep the Read-the-PNG visual check as the last line of defence.
- **Always read `deployed_url` from Supabase, never reconstruct the URL from the slug.** On Vercel, project names truncate at ~35 characters, so any slug longer than that produces a *truncated* subdomain (e.g. `aartisan-heating-plumbing-chelmsford` deploys to `aartisan-heating-plumbing-chelmsfor.vercel.app`, missing the trailing "d"). On Cloudflare Pages the same rule holds for a different reason: a global name collision appends random characters to the `pages.dev` subdomain. Either way a URL templated from the slug can 404 even though the live site is fine. When composing any message that includes the URL, copy `deployed_url` verbatim from Supabase (or the candidate-list JSON the sweep produces) — do not reconstruct it from the slug. The send-side `verify_urls_in_message` guard now suggests the real URL from Supabase when a 404 is detected, but that's a safety net — get it right at compose time.
- **Send scripts verify URLs before sending.** The send scripts check that every deployed-site URL (`vercel.app` / `pages.dev` / `netlify.app`) in the message body returns HTTP 200 before sending. If any URL is broken, the send is blocked with a clear error. This prevents sending dead links to clients. If you hit this block, fix the deployment first, then retry.
- **Read the conversation before EVERY send.** Before sending any SMS or email, read the full thread (`imessage.py read` / `gmail.py search`), then reason about whether the proposed message makes sense in context. Check for: duplicate messages already sent today, recent replies, messages from other sessions, wrong follow-up number, tone mismatches. Never send blindly from a list - even in batch operations, check each thread individually first.
- **Verify threading before sending replies.** After calling `gmail.py reply`, the email can still land as a separate thread if the `References` header is incomplete. Before sending, read the thread with `gmail.py search`, extract the `References` header from the most recent message, and pass it via `--references` with the full chain of Message-IDs appended. If in doubt, manually verify the Message-ID chain makes sense. A reply that breaks the thread looks unprofessional.
- **After stopping a background send script, always check chat.db for what actually went out.** Scripts can send messages before being killed. Assuming "stopped = nothing sent" leads to duplicates.
- **`imessage.py read` can silently lose message bodies if the regex is too tight.** On macOS Ventura+, sent iMessages frequently store *only* a control prefix (`tring+` / U+FFFC) in chat.db's legacy `text` column, while the real body lives in the `attributedBody` BLOB. A previous parser used `text or extract_text_from_attributed_body(abody)` — short-circuiting on truthy `text` — and an over-greedy regex `(?:\x00.*)?NSDictionary` that swallowed the message text along with the attribute trailer. Result: real messages rendered as bare `tring+`, which warm-leads then misclassified as tapbacks. Lesson cost: a warm lead was misclassified as needing a nudge after a 19-day gap that was actually 3 days, because the parser produced bare `tring+` instead of the real message body. Fix: anchor on `NSString\x01\x01+` + length byte and terminate on `\x02` + alpha (the real attribute trailer); always prefer `attributedBody` over `text` and only fall back to text if the BLOB yields nothing. If you ever change the parsing again, regression-test against a mix of: short messages (single-byte length), long messages (multi-byte length collapsed to `\x00` after errors=ignore), and tapbacks (no body at all).
- **Threading is a property of the recipient's mailbox, not the headers we send.** When the prospect replies from a *different* address than the one we outreach to (e.g. our chain went to `contact@business.co.uk` but they hit reply from their `business@gmail.com`), perfect `In-Reply-To` + `References` headers still won't thread on their side because their mailbox has never seen the prior Message-IDs. Symptom: our reply lands as a standalone message in the prospect's inbox. Fix: when the reply-from address differs from the original outreach-to address, **Cc the original address** so the chain stays intact at the mailbox where it lives, and the prospect still sees the new message at the address they're actively reading. `gmail.py reply` now does this automatically: it walks the `--references` chain, looks each Message-ID up in your Sent folder, and auto-Cc's any prior recipient address that differs from `--to`. There's also an orphan guard — if no prior thread can be found at all (no match at the recipient's address AND no related mailbox in references), the send aborts unless `--accept-orphan` is passed. Lesson cost: a prospect replied from Gmail to a chain originally sent to their branded mailbox; the reply orphaned because we sent only to Gmail with no CC.

## Email-first gating and Telegram noise (2026-07-24)

- **Check for a contact email BEFORE spending build tokens, not after.** The
  pipeline used to build the full site, take screenshots, and only discover
  at the outreach step that a business has no email — wasting a full build
  cycle. Fix: verify email existence during the gather/qualify stage,
  immediately after finding the business and before starting the site build.
  If no email is found there, do not proceed to build.

- **No email + strong reviews → route to Team B queue, don't kill.** A
  business with no email but a meaningful review count/rating (established,
  clearly already understands the value of being found online) is a
  candidate for the automations/systems offer (Team B, Ph1b), not a dead
  lead. Set `status=growth_lead` (or the equivalent Team B holding status)
  and note "no email, strong reviews, routed to Team B" rather than marking
  unreachable or building a free site nobody can be emailed about. Only mark
  fully `unreachable` per the existing rule (no email AND no phone AND no
  social) — routing to Team B is a distinct outcome from unreachable.

- **Do not Telegram-notify on every individual skip/kill decision.** Routine
  outcomes (no email, no phone, no social — marked unreachable; or no email
  but routed to Team B) are NOT worth a Telegram message each. These
  accumulate into the existing periodic `outreach-report.py` summary (runs
  every 3 days via cron) instead. Reserve `scripts/notify.sh` /
  `scripts/telegram-send.sh` for what already warrants it per
  `parallel-run.md`: blocked children, infra failures, 3+ consecutive
  pipeline failures, and positive replies from prospects. A single business
  being skipped for lack of contact info is not one of these — do not add a
  notify call for it.

## Disqualify at gather, not after build (2026-07-25)

- **Scratch a lead the moment it disqualifies during gather — never carry it into build.** This is the same principle as the email-first-gating rule above, generalized: no email AND no phone AND no social = unreachable, disqualify immediately. No usable photos + no reviews = too thin to build a credible site, disqualify. Under 6 months trading with no reviews (per the Nigeria sales read, 2026-07-25) = no cash flow yet, disqualify for that market. Do this check as the LAST step of gather, before build ever starts — a disqualified lead should never reach the build stage and burn tokens on a site nobody asked for and cannot be contacted about.

## Rescue Mode + Team C (enabled 2026-07-26)

Per klaudius.dev rescue-mode docs: this targets businesses whose EXISTING
website has deteriorated, not businesses with no website (that's Team A/B).
No extra config needed beyond this instruction per the docs — enable
directly in the gather stage.

### Classification: what counts as a "bad" website (rescue-mode target)
- Domain expired / doesn't resolve
- SSL certificate broken (browser warning on load)
- Parked/placeholder page ("this domain is for sale", hosting default page)
- Suspension notice from the host
- Severely obsolete: not mobile-responsive, Flash-era design, broken layout
  on a modern browser, clearly 10+ years untouched

A business with a merely dated-looking-but-functional site is NOT a rescue
target — the bar is "customer-visible failure," not "could look nicer."

### Team C — the new segment this creates
Rescue-mode leads become **Team C**: businesses with a bad/dead existing
site. Different pitch from Team A (no site) and Team B (has a fine site,
wants automations) — Team C's objection is already overcome (they already
believe in having a website, it's just broken), so the pitch leads with the
factual observation ("your current site doesn't load anymore") rather than
persuasion.

### Team C pricing (Steve's call, 2026-07-26)
- **Standard UK/general market: £399.**
- **High-ticket niches (roofing, and similarly high-transaction-value
  trades — building/construction, HVAC/heating installs, driveways):
  £700.** These businesses' average job value justifies the higher price
  and they're used to paying more for services generally.
- **African market (Nigeria/Kenya/SA): £100-£399**, scaled by company size
  and whether the niche is high-ticket locally — a small single-operator
  salon sits at the bottom of that range, an established mid-size operator
  in a higher-ticket niche (e.g. commercial cleaning, larger short-let
  operators) sits toward £399. Use judgement per lead, not a fixed split.

### Rescue-mode build note
Preserve the original site's content where recoverable (via cache/wayback/
whatever text is still extractable) plus enrich with public data (Google
Maps, reviews) — don't build from a blank slate when real existing content
can be salvaged and improved.

## QA gate + hero motion (switched on 2026-07-26)

- **Every build now requires visible motion in the hero section** — a
  static hero no longer passes QA. Use `/ui-ux-pro-max` for the motion
  design decision (what kind of motion fits this business's tone — subtle
  parallax, a slow ken-burns photo pan, a scroll-reveal, an entrance
  animation on load) and `/stitch-build:remotion` where a rendered/
  composed motion sequence is the right tool rather than pure CSS
  animation. Not every hero needs the same treatment — match the motion to
  the business (a plumber's hero moving like a luxury brand reel looks
  wrong; subtle and clean fits trades, more expressive motion fits
  higher-ticket/lifestyle businesses like Airbnb hosts or salons).
- **QA must explicitly check for hero motion before marking a build
  passed.** Add this as a hard QA gate alongside the existing photo-gallery
  void check and font-loading check: screenshot the hero at load and again
  1-2 seconds later — if the two frames are pixel-identical, the build
  fails QA and goes back for a motion pass, no exceptions.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Any hero animation must degrade to
  a static state for that media query — accessibility isn't optional
  because a build looks flashier without it.
