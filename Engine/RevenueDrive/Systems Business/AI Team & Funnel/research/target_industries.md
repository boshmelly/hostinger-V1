# Target Industries — RevenueDrive Audit, UK Market

## Selection criteria

A target industry must satisfy 4 of 5:
1. Average ticket value over £500 per job or contract
2. Operational chaos common (manual scheduling, inbox-driven ops)
3. Owner-led businesses dominant (not corporate-heavy)
4. Money present (turnover £500k to £10m bracket)
5. Resistance to change yet pain of leakage felt monthly

## Top 5 (test these in the £300 ad)

### 1. Construction main contractors and Tier 2/3 subcontractors

- UK construction has ~870,000 SMEs (15.8% of all UK businesses, the largest sector)
- Tier 2/3 subs typically £2m to £10m turnover
- Owners often founder-led, age 50+, allergic to admin software
- Pain: missed callbacks from main contractors, slow quoting, programme coordination chaos
- Ticket fit: £3k audit easily justified by one recovered subcontract

Sources: GOV.UK Business Population Estimates 2025

### 2. Independent estate agencies (2 to 15 branches)

- Real Estate Activities sector L showed significant business count increase
- Owner-led model is dominant outside corporate chains (Foxtons, Savills excluded)
- Pain: missed inbound enquiries, slow valuation follow-up, lost listings to competitors
- Owner age trend: 45+ in independent owner-operator segment
- Ticket fit: one recovered listing = £4k+ commission, justifies fee

### 3. Manufacturing SMEs

- Manufacturing accounts for 9% of SME turnover
- Family-owned dominant in primary and construction-adjacent manufacturing
- Owner age skew: 55+ commonly, succession often unplanned (16% have no plan, 69% no documented succession)
- Pain: order intake bottlenecks, quote turnaround, inbox overload, ERP underuse
- Ticket fit: one recovered B2B contract pays for the audit 5 to 50x over

### 4. M&E, plumbing, HVAC mid-tier specialist firms

- Sit between trades and construction
- Often £1m to £5m turnover
- Paper-driven scheduling still common
- Owner-led, age 45+ commonly
- Pain: missed calls during job hours, no follow-up, double-booking, quote paper trails

### 5. Independent car dealerships (5 to 30 staff)

- Owner-led, often founder + family running it
- Manual lead intake despite spending £10k+ a month on ads
- Pain: lead-to-test-drive conversion, no attribution, follow-up dies after 24 hours
- Ticket fit: one recovered car sale = £500 to £3,000 margin, audit pays back fast

## Secondary 3 (smaller test, validate)

### 6. Civil engineering consultancies

- Slow billing cycles, document chaos, founder-partner model
- Mid-£m turnover band
- Pain: project comms across stakeholders, scope creep tracking

### 7. Independent accountancy practices

- Partner age 50+ common
- Manual workflows despite tech availability
- Regulatory pressure increases urgency
- Pain: client onboarding, query backlog, billing leakage

### 8. Logistics SMEs and freight brokers

- Family-owned dominant
- Dispatch chaos, manual track-and-trace
- Pain: load-board inefficiency, customer comms, payment chasing

## Cut from consideration

- SaaS companies (saturated, sceptical of AI audits, price-sensitive)
- Marketing agencies (we'd be selling to peers, low trust)
- Pure ecommerce DTC (different model, different pain, doesn't match Ola's credibility)
- Enterprise (over £50m turnover, procurement cycle too slow for £3k offer)
- Pre-revenue startups (no revenue to leak)

## Macro context (informs messaging)

UK SME AI adoption is at 35 to 39% by mid-2025, up from 25% in 2024. Top barrier: skills gap (35%), then cost (30%), then ROI uncertainty (25%). For owner-led SMEs over 45, cultural resistance is the dominant barrier, not budget.

This means: lead with ROI proof, not technology. Lead with revenue, not features. Position as "we run this for you" not "we teach you AI."

Sources: GOV.UK AI Adoption Research 2025, Sharp UK SME survey 2025, British Chambers of Commerce 2025
