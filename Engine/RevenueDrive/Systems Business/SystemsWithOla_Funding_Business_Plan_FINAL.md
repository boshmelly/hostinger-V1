## Executive Summary

SystemsWithOla is a done-for-you AI voice and automation service for UK trades and contractor SMEs. When a client cannot answer the phone because they are on site, an AI voice agent answers in their business name, captures the caller's name, number, and job details, and sends the owner an immediate WhatsApp or SMS notification. An automated follow-up sequence contacts the caller and holds the lead. A CRM logs every enquiry.

The problem is measurable. UK trades businesses lose an average of £24,000 per year to missed calls (Digital X Marketing, 2024). Research across 13,175 calls to 45 contractor businesses found 74.1% went unanswered during normal working hours: one person cannot lay cable and answer the phone simultaneously. When a call goes unanswered, 85% of callers do not leave a voicemail. Leads contacted within five minutes are 21 times more likely to convert than those contacted after 30 minutes (InsideSales.com, 15,000-plus leads). The average UK SME takes 47 hours to respond.

## 2. The Market Problem

A typical SystemsWithOla client runs a four-person electrical contracting business in Essex. Turnover sits between £280,000 and £450,000 per year. He starts at 7am and is on site until 5pm. His phone rings multiple times a day from people who found him on Google, on Checkatrade, through a referral, or from a van wrap on the A12. He cannot answer. He is in a consumer unit. His team are doing the same.

By lunchtime, the missed calls have moved on. The leads have already called the next business on the list.

The data is consistent across multiple studies. Analysis of 13,175 inbound calls to 45 contractor businesses found that 74.1% went unanswered during normal working hours (Resonate AI / Oreate AI, 2025). Aira.io puts the figure at 62% across a broader sample of home service businesses. UBC Flexible Offices found that almost half of UK SMEs leave calls unanswered during normal business hours.

When a call goes unanswered, the lead does not wait. Seventy-nine percent of callers move to the next listing within 30 minutes if they have not spoken to a professional. Eighty-five percent will not leave a voicemail. Leads contacted within five minutes are 21 times more likely to convert than leads contacted after 30 minutes (InsideSales.com, 15,000-plus leads). A Harvard Business Review study of 2.24 million leads found that firms contacting a prospect within one hour are nearly seven times more likely to qualify the lead than those who wait. Calling within one minute of enquiry produces a 391% increase in conversion rates (Velocify, 3.5 million leads). The average UK SME takes 47 hours to respond.

The financial consequence is direct. Digital X Marketing's research puts average annual revenue loss per UK trades business from missed calls at £24,000. For roofing contractors specifically, the same 45-contractor call study found that 10.6% of unanswered calls were quote requests on projects averaging £15,000, producing an estimated £252,000 per year in lost revenue opportunity per business. UK businesses collectively lose an estimated £30 billion per year to missed calls (Paperclip).

The problem is not poor customer service. One person cannot answer the phone, complete an installation, price a quote, and follow up last week's enquiries simultaneously. Without a receptionist, and most four-person trades businesses cannot afford one, every hour on site is an hour of missed revenue. The average UK small business also loses 71 working days per year to admin at an estimated annual cost of £35,600 (Sage UK, 2025). This is structural, consistent, and unsolved at the price point the trades sector can afford.

## 3. The Solution

SystemsWithOla installs and manages three things: a call-handling layer so no lead goes unacknowledged, an instant notification system so the owner knows within seconds when a lead arrives, and a follow-up sequence that holds the lead until it either books or drops out.

When a call comes in and the owner cannot answer, the AI voice agent picks up, answers in the business name, and captures the caller's name, number, and job description. That information reaches the owner via WhatsApp or SMS within seconds. A separate automated follow-up sequence simultaneously contacts the caller, acknowledges their enquiry, and sets the expectation that the owner will be in touch shortly. If the owner has not followed up by a set trigger point, a reminder goes out. The lead is logged in the CRM so nothing falls through the gap.

The setup is entirely done-for-you. The client does not log into software, configure settings, or learn any new system. SystemsWithOla handles the full implementation: call routing, voice agent configuration, WhatsApp automation, CRM setup, and testing. The system goes live within 30 minutes of final configuration. The owner receives a simple walkthrough, not a technical manual. From that point, the system runs in the background.

Ongoing management is included in the monthly subscription. If the client needs a change, a new service area, an updated out-of-hours message, or a revised follow-up sequence, they message Ola and it is handled.

**Proof of concept:** Gerry runs an HVAC business in Essex. Before SystemsWithOla, he was converting two to three jobs per week, with missed calls his biggest identified source of lost work. After implementation, his inbound lead capture rose materially within the first month. He now books six to eight jobs per week. The revenue uplift is approximately £3,200 per month against a service cost of £350. The return-on-investment calculation is straightforward.

The infrastructure runs on enterprise-grade SaaS platforms: Salesforce for CRM, Zapier for workflow automation, and ElevenLabs for AI voice synthesis. SystemsWithOla's value is not in the underlying technology. It is in the trades-specific configuration, the automation design built from nine years of sector knowledge, and the ongoing management that means the client never has to think about it.

## 5. Market Opportunity

There are approximately 870,000 SMEs operating in UK construction and trades, the largest sector by SME count in the UK, representing 16% of the entire SME population (ONS / Statista Business Population Estimates 2024). The FSB estimates that 74% of all UK SMEs are sole traders or businesses with no employees. In construction and trades specifically, over 85% of businesses operate without dedicated admin or reception staff.

The initial target niche is the electrician market. IBISWorld data for 2024 records 48,993 registered electrician businesses in the UK, growing at 2.3% year-on-year. The comparable figure for plumbing, heating, and air conditioning is 44,630 businesses. These two segments are the immediate addressable market: concentrated, reachable through specific directories (Checkatrade, Rated People, NIC EIC registers), and sharing the same core pain point.

The niche progression is deliberate. Electricians are the entry point because the job profile matches the problem precisely. They are site-based, physically unable to take calls, regulated in ways that create credibility requirements favouring professional service over the cheapest option, and operating at a revenue scale where £250 to £350 per month is a justifiable investment if it converts one extra job per week. Roofers follow in the mid-term: average job values are higher (averaging £15,000 per the call study above), the missed-call problem is more severe, and the returns to lead capture are proportionally larger. Facilities management and block management companies represent a longer-term step-change in account value, with businesses managing multiple sites needing more sophisticated automation stacks.

Moneypenny demonstrates that this market pays for professional call handling at scale. It handles over 20 million calls per year for approximately 21,000 businesses, employs more than 1,000 staff, and generates revenues of approximately $369 million. It is backed by ECI Partners and has delivered double-digit revenue growth consistently. The market is real. Moneypenny proved it.

What Moneypenny did not do is serve the four-person electrical contractor in Romford. Its pricing starts at approximately £160 per month for an AI Voice Agent add-on, with real-world costs typically reaching £300 to £500 per month once call volume and out-of-hours coverage are factored in, and that is before any WhatsApp integration or follow-up automation, neither of which Moneypenny offers. Its model is built for established SMEs and enterprise clients. Sole traders and micro-businesses are priced out.

## 8. Go-to-Market Strategy

The first clients will not come from advertising. They will come from direct outreach to named businesses whose problem is already visible: trades businesses appearing on Google with a phone number, Checkatrade reviews mentioning response time, or active listings without a guaranteed callback promise.

The first six months use four outreach channels in combination. Cold email to registered electricians and contractors in Essex and outer London. Cold calling focused on the missed-call cost rather than the technology. LinkedIn outreach to sole traders and small contractor directors. Physical site visits to businesses with active work underway, identifiable from roadwork and building permits. Ola's TfL and highways background makes site-based conversations natural. This is not a tech salesperson walking onto a construction site. It is someone who has spent nine years coordinating the exact environment the prospect works in.

The conversion mechanism is a free seven-day trial with no technical effort required from the prospect. A trades owner who sees, for one week, that missed calls are being caught and logged, who receives WhatsApp notifications of leads while on site, and who gets a count of how many enquiries would otherwise have been lost, has a compelling reason to continue. The trial is the closing argument.

Phase One (months one and two) targets seven pilot clients in the Essex and outer London electrician market, managed closely with direct founder contact and iterative refinement of the onboarding process. Phase Two (months three to six) uses case studies and data from the pilot cohort to widen outreach to 20 clients, introducing the roofer segment and refining follow-up sequences based on live performance data. Phase Three (months seven to twelve) targets 50 clients using continued direct outreach, referral incentives for existing clients, and limited paid digital campaigns on Checkatrade and MyBuilder to supplement inbound volume.

Content marketing is not the primary Year 1 channel. Ola maintains a LinkedIn presence documenting real outcomes: jobs booked from automated follow-ups, client revenue increases, before-and-after comparisons. This is credibility infrastructure that makes each cold email and cold call faster to close.

## 10. Risk and Mitigation

**Risk 1: Client acquisition takes longer than projected.**
The sales cycle for trades SMEs is relationship-dependent. A sole trader who has never paid for an automation service may need multiple touchpoints before committing. Three mitigations apply: the free seven-day trial removes the financial risk from the first commitment; outreach volume of 50-plus contacts per week across cold email, calling, and LinkedIn creates enough pipeline that even a low conversion rate produces target acquisition numbers; and Ola's sector credibility shortens the trust-building phase materially compared with a generalist salesperson.

**Risk 2: Technology failure or AI voice quality issues.**
If the AI voice agent underperforms in call quality, lead capture accuracy, or conversational naturalness, clients will churn. In a niche market, word-of-mouth impact is disproportionate. Mitigation: a thorough test protocol before each client goes live; weekly call review in the early client cohort; and use of ElevenLabs, which has an established commercial track record in professional voice synthesis. Ola's technical competency means issues are diagnosed and resolved without dependency on external support.

**Risk 3: Competition intensifies at this price point.**
ARROW and similar lightweight UK competitors may develop additional features. Moneypenny may target the SME trades segment more aggressively. Mitigation: client stickiness through programme management depth. Not just the technology, but the relationship, the ongoing optimisation of automations, and the CRM infrastructure embedded in how the client business operates. A client who has used SystemsWithOla's CRM and follow-up sequences for six months faces material operational disruption to switch to a cheaper tool. That switching cost is the moat.

**Risk 4: Founder capacity constraint.**
In Year 1, the business is founder-operated. If client acquisition exceeds projections while delivery quality suffers, the reputation risk is severe in a word-of-mouth niche. Mitigation: a hard cap of four new client onboardings per week, with a waiting list mechanism for overflow. The working capital reserve in the funding allocation can be used to bring in part-time delivery support if capacity is reached earlier than planned.
