**Create a new Gmail.** Here is why it matters at your scale:

Your personal Gmail is attached to your personal identity, bank accounts, old jobs, subscriptions, everything. A separate Gmail keeps the content brand clean. If you ever bring in a VA, editor, or manager, you do not hand over your personal email. It also signals intentionality — [systemswithmelly]@gmail.com or [thefailurearchive]@gmail.com tells YouTube and any future sponsor this is a business operation, not a side hobby.

## YouTube Channel Name Ideas — Storytelling Niche

Psychological framing is the key. The best channel names trigger a curiosity gap, imply insider access, or create a slight discomfort that the viewer wants to resolve. Each name below is grounded in your brand themes.

### Tier A — Strongest (Psychological + Storytelling + Systems)

1. **The Operator Files** — "Files" implies classified or insider access. "Operator" is premium and signals someone who executes, not just talks.
2. **Untold Blueprint** — "Untold" triggers the curiosity gap immediately. "Blueprint" promises a system behind the story.
3. **The Rebuild Diaries** — Journalistic, vulnerable, transformation arc. Speaks to anyone rebuilding anything.
4. **Pattern of Loss** — Unsettling in the best way. Loss + pattern = the stories nobody documents but everyone recognises.
5. **The Compound Failure** — Paradox triggers interest. Failure is usually seen as singular. Compound implies it builds. That is a real phenomenon nobody talks about.
6. **Signals & Static** — Abstract but elegant. The signal is what matters. The static is everything else. Strong metaphor for your data governance background.
7. **The Quiet Operator** — Premium positioning. Suggests winning without performing. Exactly your brand.
8. **The Hidden Build** — "Hidden" triggers curiosity. Implies the real work happens where nobody looks.
9. **Dark Side of Systems** — Polarity works. Systems are usually sold as solutions. The dark side is what happens when they fail or never existed.
10. **The Failure Archive** — "Archive" gives it authority. Implies a collection, not a single story. Channel name with built-in series logic.

### Tier B — Strong (Storytelling + Business Reality)

11. **Untold Narratives** — Clean, honest label. Broad enough to cover all your pillars.
12. **Built in Silence** — Speaks to the people building without audience, applause, or external validation.
13. **Between the Shifts** — Career story angle. The things that happen in the margins of a 9-5 life.
14. **The Systems Vault** — Vault implies value stored and protected. Strong for a channel that breaks down frameworks.
15. **Rebuilt From Zero** — Clear transformation arc. Universal appeal across career, business, and personal stories.
16. **The Blind Spot Files** — Every story you have involves someone missing something they should have seen. This names it.
17. **Compound Intelligence** — Intelligence that builds on itself over time. Your whole career is this story.
18. **The Pivot Stories** — Clean, direct, searchable. Every career pivot you have made is an episode.
19. **After the 9-5** — Speaks directly to the audience you are building for. The people still in it who want out.
20. **The Feedback Loop** — Systems language. Also describes how every failure contains the information that fixes it.

### Tier C — Niche-Specific (Trades, AI, Construction, Property)

21. **The Back Office Breakdown** — Strong for the trades and small business audience specifically.
22. **Two Vans and a Vision** — Trades-specific. Grounded. Story of growth.
23. **The Data Told the Truth** — Data governance + storytelling. Your £2m AI disaster lives here.
24. **The Missed Call** — Painfully specific to your trades niche. Every electrician has lost a job this way.
25. **The Programme Files** — Nods to your programme coordinator background. Also sounds like classified documents.

### Recommendation

**The Operator Files** or **The Failure Archive** — both have series logic built in, psychological pull, and flex across all your content pillars. Neither ties you to one niche if you expand. Both sound like they could be a Netflix docuseries, which is the standard for good faceless storytelling channels.
