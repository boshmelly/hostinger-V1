## 1. Executive Summary

SystemsWithOla is a done-for-you automation business serving UK trades and contractor SMEs — electricians, roofers, plumbers, and facilities managers who are losing revenue every day because they cannot answer the phone while on site. The business installs and manages an AI voice agent that answers missed calls, captures lead data, sends the owner an immediate WhatsApp or SMS notification, and triggers an automated follow-up sequence to the caller. It also implements the back-office CRM infrastructure most small trades businesses have never had. This is not a software product sold off a shelf. It is a fully managed service delivered by a qualified programme manager with nine years of direct experience coordinating contractors in high-stakes infrastructure environments.

The problem is well-documented. UK trades businesses lose an average of £24,000 per year to missed calls (Digital X Marketing, 2024). Research across 13,175 calls from 45 contractors found that 74.1% of calls go unanswered during normal working hours — not because owners are negligent, but because they are on site doing the job they are paid to do. When a lead calls and gets no answer, 85% do not leave a voicemail. They call the next business on the list.

The founder, Ola Seweje, is APM-qualified and served as Programme Coordination Manager at Transport for London's Capital Construction directorate, where she managed multi-contractor highway programmes worth billions of pounds. She has coordinated Thames Water, UKPN, and highway maintenance contractors across pan-London programmes. She is not a tech founder who discovered the trades sector through a podcast. She has spent nine years in it, which is the basis of the product design and the credibility that opens doors with the target client.

Pricing is structured as a one-time setup fee of £500 and monthly recurring tiers of £250 (Starter), £350 (Growth), and £450 (Premium). Year 1 revenue targets are £24,000 to £48,000, with break-even projected at month four or five. The launch budget is approximately £700, drawn from personal funds, with a lean initial infrastructure of proven SaaS tools.

This document accompanies an application for grant or loan funding. The funding requested will accelerate client acquisition, formalise the technology stack, and fund the operational capacity to deliver the service to an initial cohort of 20 paying clients by the end of month six and 50 by the end of Year 1.

## 3. The Market Problem

The core client — call him Paul — runs a four-person electrical contracting business in Essex. His turnover sits between £280,000 and £450,000 per year. He starts at 7am. He is on site until 5pm. His phone rings multiple times a day from people who found him on Google, on Checkatrade, through a referral, or from a van wrap they spotted on the A12. He cannot answer. He is under a floorboard, in a consumer unit, or talking to a client. His team are doing the same.

By the time Paul checks his phone at lunchtime, the voicemails are thin and the missed calls are not going to ring back. The caller has already found someone else. Research conducted across 13,175 inbound calls to contractor businesses found that 74.1% went unanswered during normal working hours (Resonate AI / Oreate AI). Aira.io corroborates this with a figure of 62% of inbound calls to home service businesses going unanswered across a larger sample. UBC Flexible Offices found that almost half of UK SMEs leave calls unanswered during normal business hours.

When a call goes unanswered, the lead does not wait. Seventy-nine percent of callers move to the next listing within 30 minutes if they have not spoken to a professional. Eighty-five percent will not leave a voicemail. The research on speed-to-lead conversion is unambiguous: leads contacted within five minutes are 21 times more likely to convert than leads contacted after 30 minutes (InsideSales.com, analysis of 15,000-plus leads). A Harvard Business Review study of 2.24 million leads found that firms contacting a prospect within one hour are nearly seven times more likely to qualify the lead than those who wait. The average UK SME takes 47 hours to respond. By then, the job has gone.

The financial consequence is direct and measurable. Digital X Marketing's research puts the average annual revenue loss per UK trades business from missed calls at £24,000. For roofing specifically, analysis of inbound call data found that 10.6% of unanswered calls were for quote requests on projects averaging £15,000, producing an estimated £252,000 per year in lost revenue opportunity per roofing business. Even at the conservative end of the scale, a sole-trader electrician missing one qualified lead per day loses more each year than his cheapest van is worth.

The problem is not laziness or poor customer service. It is physics. One person cannot answer the phone, complete an installation, price up a quote, and follow up on last week's enquiries at the same time. Without a receptionist — and most four-person trades businesses do not have one, because the margin does not support it — every hour on site is an hour of missed revenue.

Paul typically spends between seven and nine in the evening on admin: catching up on messages, sending quotes, chasing materials. He is tired. He has a family. The admin happens in the gap between dinner and bedtime, and it is grinding. According to Sage UK research (2025), the average small business loses the equivalent of 71 working days per year to admin at an estimated cost of £35,600 annually. That is not a sustainable position for a business trying to grow.

UK SMEs collectively lose an estimated £30 billion per year to missed calls (Paperclip, UK call answering research). Across the 870,000 trades and construction SMEs operating in the UK, the addressable pain is not marginal. It is structural, consistent, and unsolved at the price point these businesses can afford.

## 4. The Solution

SystemsWithOla installs and manages three things: a call-handling layer that means no lead ever goes unacknowledged, a notification system so the owner knows immediately when a lead comes in, and a follow-up sequence that nurtures the lead until it either books or drops out.

When a call comes in and the owner cannot answer, an AI voice agent picks up. It answers in the business's name, sounds natural and professional, captures the caller's name, number, and the nature of the job. That information is then sent directly to the owner via WhatsApp or SMS — within seconds, not hours. A separate automated follow-up sequence reaches out to the caller via WhatsApp or SMS, acknowledges their enquiry, and sets the expectation that the owner will be in touch shortly. If the owner has not followed up by a trigger point — say, two hours — a reminder goes out. The lead is logged in a CRM so nothing falls through the gap between catching the message and actually making the callback.

The setup is done-for-you. The client does not log into any software, configure any settings, or learn any new systems. SystemsWithOla handles the full implementation: call routing, voice agent configuration, WhatsApp automation build, CRM setup, and testing. The whole system goes live within thirty minutes of final configuration. The owner receives a simple onboarding walkthrough — not a technical manual — and from that point, the system runs in the background.

Ongoing management is included in the monthly subscription. If something needs changing — a new service area, a different out-of-hours message, an updated follow-up sequence — the client messages Ola and it is handled.

The service is priced at three tiers. The Starter tier at £250 per month covers AI call answering, WhatsApp/SMS owner notification, and basic lead capture. The Growth tier at £350 per month adds automated follow-up sequences and a CRM implementation. The Premium tier at £450 per month includes everything in Growth plus back-office workflow automation, reporting, and priority support. All tiers include the £500 one-time setup fee.

The practical result of this service can be illustrated by a real outcome. Gerry runs an HVAC business in Essex. Before SystemsWithOla, he was converting two to three jobs per week. Missed calls were his biggest source of lost work — he knew leads were falling through but had no way to catch them without hiring a receptionist he could not afford. After implementation, his inbound lead capture rose significantly. Within the first month, he was booking six to eight jobs per week. The revenue uplift was approximately £3,200 per month. At £350 per month for the service, the return-on-investment calculation is not complicated.

The infrastructure behind the service is built on established, enterprise-grade SaaS platforms: Salesforce for CRM, Zapier for workflow automation, and ElevenLabs for AI voice generation. These are not experimental tools. They are used by businesses with tens of thousands of customers. SystemsWithOla's value is not in the underlying technology — it is in the configuration, the trades-specific knowledge embedded in the automation design, and the ongoing management that means the client never has to think about it.

## 5. Market Opportunity

There are approximately 870,000 SMEs operating in UK construction and trades — the single largest sector by SME count in the UK, representing 16% of the entire SME population (ONS / Statista Business Population Estimates 2024). Of these, the FSB estimates that 74% of all UK SMEs are sole traders or businesses with no employees. In construction and trades specifically, the figure is higher: over 85% of trades businesses operate without dedicated admin or reception staff.

The initial target niche is the electrician market. IBISWorld data for 2024 records 48,993 registered electrician businesses in the UK, a figure growing at 2.3% year-on-year. The comparable figure for plumbing, heating, and air conditioning is 44,630 businesses. These are the immediate-term addressable market — concentrated, reachable through specific directories (Checkatrade, Rated People, NIC EIC registers), and sharing the same core pain point.

The niche progression from here is deliberate. Electricians are the entry point because the job profile matches the problem most precisely: site-based, physically unable to take calls, regulated (which creates credibility requirements that favour professional service over the cheapest option), and operating at a revenue scale where £250 to £350 per month is a meaningful but justifiable investment if it converts one extra job per week. Roofers follow in the mid-term: their average job value is higher (the same contractor call study showed average roofing project values of £15,000), the call-unanswered problem is more severe, and the returns to lead capture are proportionally larger. The long-term segment — facilities management and block management companies — represents a step-change in account value, with businesses managing multiple sites and needing more sophisticated automation stacks.

Moneypenny demonstrates that this market will pay for professional call handling at scale. Moneypenny handles over 20 million calls per year for approximately 21,000 businesses, employs more than 1,000 staff, and generates revenues of approximately $369 million. It is backed by private equity (ECI Partners) and has delivered double-digit revenue growth consistently. The market is real. Moneypenny proved it.

What Moneypenny did not do is serve the four-person electrical contractor in Romford or the roofer in Basildon. Its pricing starts at approximately £160 per month for an AI Voice Agent add-on, with total real-world costs typically reaching £300 to £500 per month once call volume and out-of-hours coverage are factored in — and that is before any WhatsApp integration or follow-up automation, neither of which Moneypenny offers. Its model is built for established SMEs and enterprise clients with sufficient call volume to justify a managed human-staffed service. Sole traders and micro-businesses are priced out, or they do not fit the model.

ARROW, the closest UK competitor targeting the trades sector specifically, offers basic AI call answering from £99 per month. It does not include WhatsApp notification, follow-up sequences, or CRM integration. It is a single-feature product. Trademate automates lead responses from directories but does not answer inbound calls. None of the current market players combine all the components — call answering, instant WhatsApp notification, automated follow-up, CRM — in a done-for-you format at a mid-market price point.

That gap between Moneypenny's enterprise pricing and ARROW's basic feature set is the market SystemsWithOla is built to occupy.

## 7. Business Model and Financial Projections

The revenue model is simple: a one-time setup fee of £500 per client, followed by a monthly recurring subscription at £250, £350, or £450 depending on tier. The business expects the Growth tier (£350 per month) to be the most common choice among established tradespeople and small contractor businesses, based on the feature set it unlocks (follow-up automation and CRM access).

The cost base at launch is deliberately lean. The core SaaS infrastructure — Salesforce, Zapier, ElevenLabs — runs at under £200 per month at the outset. Delivery is founder-operated. There are no premises costs. The primary variable cost is time: client onboarding, automation configuration, and ongoing account management.

The following projections use three scenarios. The conservative case assumes slower sales cycles and a higher proportion of Starter-tier clients. The realistic case reflects a steady acquisition rate with a mix of tiers weighted toward Growth. The optimistic case assumes the pilot client referral loop activates early and Growth/Premium adoption is higher.

**Client and Revenue Assumptions:**

The target is seven pilot clients onboarded in months one and two, 20 clients by end of month six, and 50 clients by end of Year 1. Churn is assumed at 5% per month after month three, consistent with typical SaaS churn for SME clients once the service has demonstrated clear value. The Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) blended across tiers is assumed at £320 per month for projection purposes.

**Break-Even Analysis:**

At an estimated monthly cost base of £800 (infrastructure and founder operating costs) in the early phase, break-even is reached when monthly recurring revenue exceeds that figure. At an ARPU of £320, this requires three paying clients. Based on the acquisition timeline, break-even is projected at month four to five, inclusive of the ramp-up period. This is a conservative position — the setup fees collected during client onboarding provide additional cash buffer in the early months.

## 8. Go-to-Market Strategy

The first clients will not come from advertising. They will come from direct, manual outreach to named businesses whose problem is already visible from the outside — specifically, trades businesses who appear on Google with a phone number, have reviews that mention response time issues, or are listed on Checkatrade without a guaranteed callback promise.

The outreach model in the first six months uses four channels in combination: cold email to registered electricians and contractors in Essex and outer London; cold calling (brief, direct, focused on the missed-call problem not the technology); LinkedIn outreach to sole traders and small contractor directors; and physical site visits to businesses with active work underway, which the founder can identify from roadwork and building permits. The founder's TfL and highways background makes site-based conversations natural rather than awkward — this is not a tech salesperson walking onto a construction site. It is someone who has spent nine years coordinating the exact environment the prospect works in.

The conversion mechanism is a free seven-day trial with no technical effort required from the prospect. The business absorbs the setup cost on trial accounts because the conversion data — and the referral potential — justifies it. A trades owner who sees, for one week, that missed calls are being caught and logged, who receives WhatsApp notifications of leads while on site, and who gets a count of how many enquiries would otherwise have been lost, has a powerful reason to continue. The trial is the closing argument.

The acquisition sequence is structured in three phases. Phase One (months one and two) targets seven pilot clients in the Essex and outer London electrician market. This cohort is managed closely, with the founder maintaining direct contact and iterating the onboarding process based on real feedback. Phase Two (months three to six) uses the case studies and data generated from the pilot cohort to widen outreach to 20 clients, introducing the roofer segment and refining the WhatsApp follow-up sequences based on live performance data. Phase Three (months seven to twelve) targets 50 clients total, using a combination of continued direct outreach, referral incentives for existing clients, and a limited paid digital campaign in the trades directories (Checkatrade, MyBuilder) to supplement inbound volume.

Content marketing is not the primary channel in Year 1 — there is not enough time or budget to build an audience from scratch while simultaneously running client delivery. However, the founder will maintain a LinkedIn presence that documents real outcomes: jobs booked from automated follow-ups, client revenue increases, specific before-and-after comparisons. This is not brand building for its own sake. It is credibility infrastructure that makes the cold email and cold call easier to close.

For a Start Up Loan funder, the repayment position is straightforward. At 50 clients and £16,000 per month in recurring revenue by end of Year 1, a £15,000 to £20,000 loan repaid over three years at 7.5% carries a monthly repayment of approximately £465 to £621 — well within the revenue envelope at that scale.

## 10. Risk and Mitigation

**Risk 1: Client acquisition takes longer than projected.** The sales cycle for trades SMEs is relationship-dependent and can be slow. A sole trader who has never paid for an automation service before may need multiple touchpoints before committing. The mitigation is threefold: the free seven-day trial removes the financial risk from the first commitment; the outreach volume (50-plus contacts per week across cold email, calling, and LinkedIn) creates enough pipeline that even a low conversion rate produces the target acquisition numbers; and the founder's sector credibility shortens the trust-building phase materially compared to a generalist salesperson.

**Risk 2: Technology failure or AI voice quality issues.** The AI voice agent is the central product, and if it underperforms — in call quality, accuracy of lead capture, or naturalness of conversation — the client will churn and the word-of-mouth impact in a niche market will be negative. The mitigation is a thorough test protocol before each client goes live, an ongoing monitoring process (calls are reviewed weekly in the early client cohort), and the use of ElevenLabs, a platform with an established commercial track record in professional voice synthesis. The founder's technical competency means issues can be diagnosed and resolved without dependency on external support.

**Risk 3: Competition intensifies at the price point.** ARROW and similar lightweight UK competitors may develop additional features. Moneypenny may reduce pricing to target the SME trades segment more aggressively. The mitigation is to build client stickiness through programme management depth — not just the technology, but the relationship, the ongoing optimisation of automations, and the CRM infrastructure that becomes embedded in how the business operates. A client who has used SystemsWithOla's CRM and follow-up sequences for six months is not going to switch to a cheaper tool without operational disruption. The switching cost is the moat.

**Risk 4: Founder capacity constraint.** In Year 1, the business is founder-operated. If client acquisition exceeds projections while delivery quality suffers, the reputation risk is severe in a word-of-mouth-driven niche market. The mitigation is a hard cap on new client onboarding — no more than four new clients per week — with a waiting list mechanism to manage overflow. The funding allocation includes a working capital reserve that can be used to bring in part-time delivery support if capacity is reached earlier than planned.
