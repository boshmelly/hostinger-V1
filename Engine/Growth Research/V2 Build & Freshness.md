# V2 Build — Expert Team Output & Freshness Check
### 18yr+ advisory team · 28 June 2026


## Sales Director (18yr B2B + agency)
Owner-led 45+ SMEs with weak digital don't buy "digital growth" or "partnership" as concepts — they buy relief from a specific, visible problem and respect for what they've already built. V1 (sell digital) leads with a concrete, named gap the owner can see for themselves (a competitor outranking them, a dead Google listing, leads going unanswered); V2 (partner/grow/acquire) leads with succession and legacy — "you built something real, what happens to it next." Both sequences are short, human, and engineered to earn a 15-minute call, not to pitch in writing.

- V1 best angle (sell digital): the named-competitor gap. Email 1 cites ONE specific, verifiable thing you found in 90 seconds of looking ('searched [their service] in [town] last Tuesday — three competitors show up before you, including [Name]'). Specificity proves you actually looked at them, which 45+ owners read as respect, not spam. Never lead with 'we do SEO/websites/social.'
- V2 best angle (partner/grow/acquire): succession and legacy, not 'growth.' This owner is 45+, has run it 15-20 years, and is quietly wondering what happens when they slow down. Lead with 'you've built something that works — most owners your stage haven't thought about what's next for it, and the ones who wait lose the choice.' You're offering continuity and an exit on their terms, not a takeover.
- Sequence (both doorways): Email 1 (Day 0, the angle + one specific observation, soft ask) -> Email 2 (Day 4, the proof/mini-case of a peer like them, re-ask) -> Email 3 (Day 9, the 'breakup'/permission email, lowest-friction ask) -> Call (Day 11-12, warm because they've now seen your name 3x). Total 4 touches over ~12 days. Phone is the closer, not the opener — email earns the right to call.
- Cadence rule: space touches 4-5 days apart, not daily. This cohort checks email in batches and reads daily follow-up as pestering. The Day 9 breakup email ('I'll assume the timing's wrong and stop here — fair?') is the single highest-reply touch in the whole sequence; it converts the silent 'maybe' pile.
- Call opener (both): permission + their world first. 'I sent you a couple of notes about [specific thing] — did I catch you at an OK moment, or should I call back?' Then talk about THEIR business for the first 2 minutes before anything about you. For V1 the call goal is a screen-share audit booked; for V2 it's a 'no-strings conversation about your options in 12-24 months.'
- V1 vs V2 routing: lead with V1 (sell digital) for owners still clearly building/competing; lead with V2 (partner) for owners 55+, no obvious successor, or category leaders who've plateaued. When unsure, open with V1 — it's a smaller ask and a digital engagement is the natural on-ramp that earns the trust to later raise V2.
- Proof must be peer-shaped, not metric-shaped. '[Name], who runs a [same trade] two towns over' beats '+340% traffic.' This owner trusts people like them over dashboards. One named, relatable peer story per sequence — same age bracket, same kind of business, ideally same region.
- Disqualify fast and say so. Make every email easy to say 'no, not now' to — it raises reply rates and protects sender reputation. A clean 'not interested' from 100 is worth more than 100 ignores; you can re-approach a 'not now' in 6 months, you can't re-approach a spam flag.

**Email rules:**
- Length: 75-120 words max, email 1. If it scrolls on a phone, it's too long. The owner reads on a cracked iPhone between jobs — one screen, no scroll.
- One specific, verifiable observation per email that proves you looked at THEIR business (a competitor name, their actual Google rating, a broken contact form, a dead Facebook page). Generic = deleted. This is the single biggest reply-rate lever.
- Plain language, zero jargon. Banned words: 'digital transformation,' 'synergy,' 'leverage,' 'solutions,' 'cutting-edge,' 'ROI,' 'omnichannel,' 'scale,' 'leverage your assets.' Write like one business owner talking to another over coffee. If a 45-year-old plumber wouldn't say it out loud, cut it.
- Subject lines: lowercase or sentence case, 3-6 words, specific and curiosity-led, never salesy. Good: 'quick thing about [town] search,' 'noticed this on your site,' '[Competitor] showing above you.' Banned: ALL CAPS, exclamation marks, 'FREE,' 'limited time,' '🚀,' anything that smells like a blast.
- One ask per email, and make it tiny. Email 1 ask = a yes/no question or '15 minutes,' never 'book a demo' or a calendar wall. Lower the cost of replying at every step. The Day 9 ask should be the easiest of all ('want me to stop here?').
- Respect the legacy, never the deficit. Frame gaps as missed opportunity ('you're leaving leads on the table'), never as failure ('your website is outdated/bad'). This owner built the business with their hands — insulting the current state ends the conversation. Acknowledge what works before naming what's missing.
- For V2 specifically: never use the words 'acquire,' 'buyout,' or 'exit' in email 1 — they trigger defensiveness. Use 'what's next,' 'options,' 'continuity,' 'on your terms.' Open the door to a conversation, don't put a deal on the table in writing.
- Always include a clean, dignified opt-out in follow-ups ('if this isn't useful, just say so and I'll leave you be'). It raises replies, protects deliverability, and reads as respect rather than desperation.
- Sign as a named human with a real direct line and a real title, never 'The [Company] Team' or a no-reply address. Plain-text feel, no heavy graphics, no tracking-pixel banners — image-heavy templates read as marketing and tank trust with this cohort.
- End every email with a forward-motion line that assumes nothing and pressures nothing: a question they can answer in three words, or a clear statement of what you'll do next ('I'll give you a quick call Thursday — wave me off if it's a bad time').

## Business-Model Architect (18yr SME/deal structuring)
V1 ranges (10-20 pct lead, 2-10 pct finder, up-to-30 pct earn-in) are too wide to quote and read as guesswork to a sceptical 45-plus owner. - 1a price by lead value not a flat band. Tier A (lead over GBP 2k: implants/dentures/surveys/legal): GBP 75-150 per qualified lead or 8-10 pct of booked-job value. Tier B (GBP 500-2k): GBP 40-75/lead or 10-12 pct. Tier C (under GBP 500): skip rev-share, use 1b free site plus care plan. Switch to per-lead pricing after 90 days of conversion data.
- 1a baseline is the fairness key. Take last 3-6 months of their enquiries as a baseline; pay ONLY on qualified leads above it, via your tracking. Without a baseline you are charging for demand the owner already had. Re-baseline yearly, upward only.
- 1a tracking (no tracking, no deal): unique call-tracking number plus form UTM attribution plus a booked/attended tag in CRM. Define qualified in writing. Cap your spend exposure, 14-day dispute window, bill monthly in arrears off a log the owner can see.
- 1c replace 2-10 pct with Lehman scale on EV: 5 pct first GBP 1m, 4 pct second, 3 pct third, 2 pct after, hard floor GBP 3-5k per completed intro. The 0.5-10m pipeline lands at 3-5 pct effective. Signed introducer agreement plus blind teaser before any identifying detail.
- 1c fairness: broker/buyer pays you, never the seller, disclosed. If a broker already pays referral commission, take their cut, do not stack a second fee or you get cut from the network. Add a 12-24 month trail clause so buyers cannot go quiet then transact direct.
- 2a rev-share: tighten to 12 pct on incremental revenue above a trailing-12-month baseline for 36 months. 10 pct if owner funds the build, 15 pct if you carry build plus marketing. Buyout: owner clears it at 1.5x trailing-12-month payments. No cap on upside, that is the alignment.
- 2a earn-in: replace up-to-30 pct with EMI options over 36 months, 1-year cliff at 10 pct then 5 pct per 6 months to a 25-30 pct cap, each tranche gated on an EBITDA milestone. Strike at today valuation. Bad-leaver forfeits unvested plus clawback. Year-3 call at 4x EBITDA matched by owner put at 4.5x.
- Sequencing: every deal opens with the free build (1b spine) so value is banked before economics. Pick one lever from the rubric: deal_fit 2 plus lead over GBP 500 means 1a; attached/asset-backed plus margin 2.5 means 2a; stated exit intent means 1c. One prospect, one lever, decided by data. ## Deal Broker (18yr M&A / business transfer)
Brokering is a paperwork-and-routing game before it's a selling game: you lock the relationship with a signed introducer/finder or sole-mandate agreement, control information flow through a blind teaser then a gated CIM, and match the seller to the right buyer pool or sub-broker. The differentiator on smaller UK/Irish deals is wrapping government funding (Growth Guarantee Scheme, LEO Trading Online Voucher, Made Smarter) around the transaction so the buyer's acquisition/transition is part-funded and the seller carries almost no cash risk to get to completion.

- INTRODUCER vs MANDATE — know which you are selling. An introducer/finder agreement only pays you for the named introduction (one-line scope: 'X% of consideration on a deal completing with a party I introduce, tail period 18-24 months, named-parties schedule attached'). A sole-mandate/engagement agreement gives you exclusivity, a retainer, and a success fee on ANY buyer. Never work a sellable business on a bare finder note — you'll get circumvented. Always include: defined 'Introduced Party' list, tail/runoff clause, definition of 'Consideration' (cash + deferred + earn-out + assumed debt + retained property), and a circumvention/non-circumvention clause.
- FEE MECHANICS — make it concrete. Typical sub-£2m deal: a modest monthly retainer or upfront work fee (credited against success) to weed out tyre-kickers, plus a success fee on a Lehman-style or flat scale (e.g. 5-10% on lower-mid market, higher % on the first tranche). State WHEN it's earned ('on exchange') vs WHEN paid ('on completion / on receipt of each deferred tranche'). For earn-outs, get paid on the headline structure value or take a slice of each tranche as it lands — decide upfront and write it in.
- BLIND TEASER PROCESS — control information in stages. Stage 1: one-page Blind Teaser / IM with NO identifying detail — sector, region (broad), revenue/EBITDA bands, headline reason for sale, asking guide. Stage 2: NDA executed by the named buyer entity (with a non-solicit of staff/customers and a no-direct-contact clause routing everything through you). Stage 3: release the full CIM/IM with financials. Stage 4: indicative offer / Heads of Terms before you open the data room. Never let a buyer skip a gate — the teaser exists precisely so you can shop the deal without the market, staff, or customers learning the business is for sale.
- ROUTING A SELLER — qualify, then place. First triage the seller: is it sellable (clean books, owner-independent, defensible margin) or a lifestyle/asset sale? Sector, deal size, and buyer type decide the route: trade buyer, PE/search fund, MBO/MBI team, or a competitor. If it's outside your lane (wrong sector, too big, too small, regulated), refer to the right broker under a written broker-to-broker fee split (typically 25-50% of the success fee) rather than running it badly. Maintain a live buyer book segmented by sector + ticket size so routing is a lookup, not a cold start.
- FUNDING WRAP — Growth Guarantee Scheme (UK). GGS is the successor to RLS: a government-backed guarantee (up to ~£2m facility) that lets accredited lenders fund acquisitions, working capital, and growth where the buyer lacks security. Use it to fund the BUYER's acquisition finance so the seller can take more cash at completion and less deferred/earn-out risk. Broker move: pre-line up 2-3 GGS-accredited lenders so a credible buyer arrives 'funding-ready'; it widens your buyer pool to people who couldn't otherwise raise the cash.
- FUNDING WRAP — LEO Trading Online Voucher (Ireland) + Made Smarter (UK). These don't fund the purchase price — they de-risk the TRANSITION and lift sale value. LEO Trading Online Voucher (Republic of Ireland, via Local Enterprise Office): up to ~€2,500 matched-funding to build/upgrade the target's e-commerce and online trading — use it to dress the business pre-sale or fund the buyer's first 90-day digital uplift. Made Smarter (UK manufacturing/SME): grants + advisory for digital tech adoption — position it as buyer value-add to fund post-completion modernisation. Both let you tell the seller 'the buyer's improvement plan is grant-funded, so your earn-out targets are realistically hittable.'
- STACK THE WRAP SO THE OWNER RISKS LITTLE. The pitch to a nervous seller: (1) my work fee is small/credited; (2) the buyer's acquisition cash is GGS-backed, so you get more at completion not just deferred; (3) the buyer's growth/transition plan is part-funded by LEO/Made Smarter grants, so the earn-out you're relying on is achievable; (4) you sign nothing identifying until an NDA is in place. Net: the owner's downside is bounded to time, and the structure shifts financing risk onto the lender/grant bodies and the buyer.
- ALWAYS CHECK CURRENT SCHEME TERMS BEFORE QUOTING. GGS caps/eligibility, LEO voucher amounts and the 50% match, and Made Smarter regional coverage all change with funding rounds and government budgets — verify limits and accredited-lender/LEO-office lists at point of use rather than promising a figure to a seller. Treat the funding wrap as a structuring tool, not a guarantee, and never present grant money as certain in Heads of Terms.

**Email rules:**
- Lead with the gate, not the business: a cold teaser email must never name the company, location to street level, or owner — sector, region band, revenue/EBITDA band, and reason for sale only.
- One clear call to action per email: 'reply to receive the NDA' or 'sign attached NDA for the full information memorandum' — never two asks.
- Keep teasers to one screen / under ~150 words; the IM does the heavy lifting after the NDA, not the email.
- Tone: discreet, confident, factual. No hype adjectives ('amazing opportunity'), no pressure tactics — buyers in this market distrust both.
- When emailing a seller, foreground risk-reduction levers in plain English (small/credited fee, buyer funding-ready via GGS, grant-funded transition) — quantify only what you can stand behind.
- Never assert a specific grant amount or guarantee approval as a fact in writing; use 'up to' and 'subject to eligibility/current scheme terms'.
- Always route buyer-seller contact through the broker: every email should reinforce 'all questions and visits via me', and restate the non-circumvention expectation politely.
- Include a clear confidentiality footer and your engagement basis; avoid forwarding financials or identifying attachments to anyone whose NDA you haven't countersigned.

## Marketing & Case-Study Strategist (18yr)
You have exactly one hard proof (Essex HVAC, +£3,200/mo) plus founder credibility. The ethical play is to lead with the *mechanism* that produced the result, not the *logo* — sell the Essex story as a worked example of a repeatable method, and let founder credibility carry the gap into sectors you haven't served yet. Never fake a same-sector client; instead say "I haven't done a [dentist] yet, here's the [HVAC] one and exactly why the same lever applies to you" — the honesty itself becomes a differentiator in an inbox full of fabricated proof.

- PROOF-OF-METHOD FORMULA: Lever -> Mechanism -> Result -> Transfer. State the specific lever you pulled (e.g. 'recovered missed-call leads'), name the mechanism (what you actually changed), cite the Essex number (+£3,200/mo), then bridge to the prospect's sector with a one-line transfer claim ('every [trade/clinic] leaks the same way'). The number is evidence of the mechanism working, never a promise of identical results.
- Sell the mechanism, not the match. In HVAC you'll say 'same trade'; in a dentist or law firm you say 'different business, identical leak.' Always name WHY the lever transfers (both miss inbound calls during jobs/appointments) so the cross-sector jump feels reasoned, not hand-waved. Lead generic in non-HVAC sectors, lead specific in HVAC-adjacent ones.
- Turn the single-client gap into a trust asset. Use a 'first in your sector' frame: 'I've proven this in HVAC; you'd be my first [pest control] case — which is why I'll [reduced rate / pilot terms / performance-linked].' This is honest, creates scarcity, and de-risks for the prospect instead of pretending you have ten lookalike clients.
- Make the number do three jobs: credible (oddly specific, £3,200 not 'thousands'), bounded (per month, recurring, not a one-off win), and attributed (tie it to the one lever, so it's defensible if challenged on a call). Never round it up, never imply it's an average across many clients.
- Deploy founder credibility as the bridge, not the headline. One concrete line about the founder's relevant background (years in/around the trade, what they built before) earns the right to make the transfer claim. It answers 'why should I trust your method with only one case?' Keep it to a single clause — it supports the proof, it doesn't replace it.
- Build a 3-tier proof ladder by sector heat: HOT (HVAC/trades) = full Essex story, same-trade framing. WARM (other local service SMBs - dentists, gyms, clinics) = Essex story + explicit 'different sector, same leak' transfer. COLD (anything unrelated) = lead with founder credibility + the mechanism, mention Essex as illustration, offer pilot terms.
- Pre-empt the 'only one client' objection inside the email before they think it. A single honest line ('Full disclosure: one documented case so far, in HVAC — happy to walk you through exactly what we changed') outperforms hiding it, because the reader was already suspicious of your proof. Honesty disarms the skepticism that fabricated case studies trigger.
- Hard ethical guardrails: never invent a client, logo, testimonial, or sector-specific result; never imply Essex represents a portfolio; never present +£3,200 as typical/guaranteed; never reuse the number with a swapped industry name. One real proof used honestly beats five invented ones the moment a prospect asks a follow-up question on a call.

**Email rules:**
- Lead with the prospect's problem (the leak), not your case study. The Essex proof appears in line 2-3 as evidence, never as the opener — nobody cares about your client until they see their own pain first.
- Use the exact figure '+£3,200/mo' — never round, never vague-up to 'thousands', never average it across imaginary clients. Specificity is the credibility; generality kills it.
- Always pair the number with its lever in the same sentence ('by recovering missed-call leads, +£3,200/mo'). A result without a stated mechanism reads as a boast; with one it reads as a method.
- Include exactly one transfer line that names why HVAC maps to the prospect's sector ('you lose calls during appointments the same way they lost them during jobs'). No transfer line = unsupported leap.
- Be explicit and unembarrassed about it being one case. One honest disclosure clause beats implied scale; it converts skepticism into trust and pre-empts the obvious objection.
- Founder credibility = one clause maximum, used as a bridge ('15 years around the trades, so I know where the money leaks'). Never let it become the email's spine or a paragraph.
- Keep it under 120 words, one ask, one specific CTA (a 10-min call or a Loom audit), never 'let me know if interested'. Low-friction, single next step.
- Never fabricate, imply, or borrow a same-sector client, logo, quote, or statistic. If you haven't done that sector, say so and offer pilot/first-client terms instead.
- Match proof depth to sector heat: full Essex story for trades, 'different sector same leak' framing for other local SMBs, mechanism + founder credibility + pilot offer for cold/unrelated sectors.
- Tone: peer-to-peer operator, not agency pitch. Plain trade language, no jargon, no superlatives ('game-changing', 'revolutionary'), no exclamation marks. Write like one business owner tipping off another.

## Buyer & Owner Psychologist (18yr behavioural)
The 45+ emotionally-attached owner is not deciding whether to sell — they are deciding whether to trust you with something they built and partly identify with. They are scanning your cold email for two things: do you see the thing as a transaction or as their life's work, and are you trying to take something or give something. Trust is won by lowering the perceived threat to their autonomy and legacy, not by raising the perceived upside.

- Lead with their legacy, not your offer. The deepest lever for this segment is continuity ('what happens to the people/name/thing I built'), which outranks price. Reference what they've built specifically before you reference any outcome you want.
- Use loss aversion as protection, not pressure. Don't manufacture FOMO ('act now / market won't last'). Instead frame around protecting what they already have ('so the years you put in don't get undervalued by someone who doesn't understand it'). Loss aversion works here as guardianship, not scarcity.
- Give before you ask (reciprocity + trust-by-giving). Open with one genuinely useful, no-strings observation or piece of value specific to them. A cold email that delivers something before requesting a call inverts the expected taker frame and disarms scepticism.
- Protect autonomy explicitly. This buyer fears being managed, rushed, or handled. Use language that hands control back: 'no obligation,' 'entirely your call,' 'whenever it suits you, or not at all.' The easier you make it to say no, the more they lower their guard.
- Earn the right to be brief. Skepticism reads length as manipulation and slickness as a trap. Short, plain, specific, and slightly understated signals confidence and honesty. Over-polish triggers 'salesperson' pattern-matching instantly.
- Name yourself as a real, accountable human. A real name, a real role, a real reason you're reaching out to THEM specifically. Anonymity and mass-mail signals are the fastest trust-killers for a 45+ skeptic.
- Match their pace and dignity. Slower cadence, respectful register, zero hype. Treat them as the expert in the room. The emotional win is feeling respected as a builder, not prospected as a lead.
- Make the next step tiny and reversible. Not 'book a 30-min discovery call' but 'a 10-minute conversation, no pitch, and if it's not useful you've lost nothing.' Low commitment + reversibility beats high-value promises for the wary.

**Email rules:**
- TONE: Plain, calm, respectful, slightly understated. Write like one competent adult to another, not marketer to lead. No exclamation marks, no hype adjectives.
- LENGTH: Short. 90-150 words, 3-5 short paragraphs. Every sentence must earn its place. Length reads as manipulation to this segment.
- OPEN: First line must show you see THEM specifically (their business/work/name), not a templated 'I hope this finds you well.' Specificity is the trust proof.
- GIVE FIRST: Include one concrete, no-strings piece of value or insight before any request. The ask comes last and small.
- AUTONOMY LANGUAGE: Use 'no obligation,' 'entirely your call,' 'only if it's useful to you,' 'or not at all.' Hand control back explicitly.
- LEGACY FRAME: Reference what they built and its continuity ('the people you've built,' 'what you've spent years on') rather than valuation or upside.
- CTA: One tiny, reversible step. 'A 10-minute call, no pitch' — never 'discovery call,' 'jump on a quick call,' or a calendar-link demand in a cold first touch.
- SIGN-OFF: Real first and last name, real role, a human line. No 'Best regards, The [Company] Team.'
- WORDS TO USE: built, years, the people, your call, no obligation, no pitch, honestly, only if useful, whenever suits, I noticed, specifically.
- WORDS/PHRASES TO AVOID: 'act now,' 'limited time,' 'don't miss,' 'exciting opportunity,' 'synergy,' 'unlock,' 'reach out,' 'touch base,' 'circle back,' 'I hope this email finds you well,' 'just following up,' 'maximise your exit,' 'leverage,' any urgency or scarcity, any flattery that isn't specific.
- AVOID: Pressure, false urgency, over-promising returns, naming a price or multiple, slick formatting, multiple CTAs, anything that implies you already know they want to sell.
- PROOF, NOT CLAIMS: If you assert credibility, attach a verifiable specific (a named comparable, a real result) — vague 'we're the best/trusted' triggers scepticism rather than easing it.
